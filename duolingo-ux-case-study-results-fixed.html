The problem

Streaks are one of Duolingo's best ideas. They turn a hard habit into a daily win. But the same mechanic can make learners feel guilty, anxious, or punished when real life gets in the way.

My question: when does a streak help someone learn, and when does it push them to quit?

What the redesign changed
Illustrative prototype resultIn the simulated 5-person test, 4/5 participants understood the rest-day concept without help, 4/5 preferred the progress-focused recovery message, and 3/5 said the gentler reminder felt less pressuring.
Before“You lost your 12-day streak. Start again from 0.”
Proposed“You know 240 words and 12 lessons. Ready for a quick one today?”

What I learned

The strongest design opportunity is not removing the streak. It is changing the missed-day moment from loss-focused to progress-focused, while giving learners more control over breaks and reminders.

What I'd do next

Run the same test with real participants, compare return-to-learning behavior over a longer period, and validate whether gentler language affects retention rather than only immediate emotion.
